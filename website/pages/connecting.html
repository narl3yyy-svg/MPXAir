USB devices are listed with a short name, for example ACM0 · Espressif. Open the list to see the full name. New devices are noticed as soon as they're plugged in.

The connection window

When you press Connect, a window shows each step, like Mission Planner's:

  1. Opened the port or address.
  2. Heartbeat received from the aircraft, with its type and system ID. This is the proof the air unit is talking to you.
  3. Parameters counting up, for example 812 / 1431.
  4. Mission, Geofence and Rally points read from the aircraft.

It closes on its own a couple of seconds after everything has loaded. Press Hide to close it early (loading carries on), or Cancel while it is still waiting for the heartbeat.

Ground station only (aircraft off)

If you connect to a ground station board (for example the RFD900 + ESP32 board) while the aircraft is switched off, MPXAir keeps the link open and says Waiting for aircraft. The ground station's GPS is shown straight away. When the aircraft powers up, MPXAir connects to it and loads its parameters automatically.

Automatic reconnect

If a USB device drops off (unplugged, or a power dip on the board), MPXAir shows Reconnecting… and waits up to two minutes. It recognises the same device by its unique USB ID, even if it comes back under a new name (for example ttyACM1 instead of ttyACM0), then reconnects and reloads everything. Press Stop to give up.

Telemetry recording

Everything received while connected is saved to a telemetry log (.tlog) in ~/Documents/MPXAir/telemetry-logs. You can replay it later. Turn this off in Preferences → General.

If it doesn't connect

  • No heartbeat within 30 seconds. Check that the aircraft is powered, that the right port is selected, and that the baud rate matches the radio (57600 for most SiK/RFD radios).
  • Permission denied. Add yourself to the dialout group (see Troubleshooting).
  • The port isn't in the list. See a USB device disappears.
